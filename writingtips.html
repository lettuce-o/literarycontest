A notebook full of possibility

Make your story yours.

You don't need a grand plan to start. Try one small moment, then follow where it leads.

01

Start with a change

Begin when something shifts: a secret is found, a plan goes wrong, or a new person arrives.

02

Give your character a want

A clear goal makes it easier for readers to care. What does your character hope for today?

03

Choose telling details

Use a few specific sights, sounds, or objects to make a scene feel real.

04

Keep it focused

For a short story, stick to one main problem and a small number of important moments.

05

Read it out loud

Your ears can catch clunky sentences, missing words, and places that need a pause.

06

Revise, then stop

Cut what distracts, keep what matters, and let your own voice show.

From a seed to a story

Try this tiny transformation.

A simple idea becomes a story when a character wants something and meets a little trouble.

The simple idea

A kid finds a key.

Add a want and a snag

Mina finds a key under the library steps. She wants to return it before the doors close—but the tiny tag says “Roof,” and the stairs are locked.

Now there's a story question

What is on the roof, and will Mina get there in time?
