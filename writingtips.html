George Orwell's 6 Rules for Writing

1. Avoid cliches

"Never use a metaphor, simile or other figure of speech which you are used to seeing in print."

2. Use simple language

"Never use a long word where a short one will do."

3. Be concise

"If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out."

4. Use active voice

"Never use the passive where you can use the active."

5. Use easily-understandable language

"Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word or a jargon word if you can think of an everyday English equivalent."

6. Don't follow these rules if they are unnatural

"Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous."

What Our Judges Look For

The Judge's Mindset

We read hundreds of entries. The stories that make the shortlist aren't necessarily the ones with the biggest plot twists—they're the ones with an unmistakable voice.

Originality of Voice

Does this sound like only you could have written it?

Emotional Resonance

Did the story make the reader feel something genuine by the final line?

Craft & Control

Pacing, sentence rhythm, and clean execution without gimmicks.

Theme Integration

A fresh, non-obvious take on the prompt.

What Makes a Good Short Story

Immediate Tension

Start as close to the ending as possible. Drop the reader directly into the conflict.

Focused Scope

A single turning point or realization rather than a sprawling timeline.

Economical Prose

Every sentence must either reveal character or advance the plot.

A Resonant Ending

Leave the reader with an emotional echo, not necessarily every loose end tied up.
